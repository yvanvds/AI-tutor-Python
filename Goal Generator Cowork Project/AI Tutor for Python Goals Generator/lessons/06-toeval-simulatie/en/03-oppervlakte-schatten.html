Throw a thousand darts blindfolded at a rectangular board with a shape drawn on it. The share of the darts that lands in the shape tells you how big the shape is.

A random point

import random

x = random.uniform(0, 4)
y = random.uniform(0, 3)
print(round(x, 2), round(y, 2))

One possible output:

2.49 2.23

random.uniform(a, b) gives a decimal number between a and b. Two such numbers form a point that lands just as easily anywhere in the rectangle of 4 by 3.

Hit or miss

For every shape you need a test that says whether a point is in it. A point is inside the circle with center (0, 0) and radius 1 if x² + y² is smaller than 1.

def in_circle(x, y):
    return x ** 2 + y ** 2 < 1

print(in_circle(0.5, 0.5))
print(in_circle(0.9, 0.8))

Output:

True
False

For the point (0.5, 0.5) the sum is 0.5: a hit. For (0.9, 0.8) it is 1.45: a miss. That is how you check for every point what the condition decides.

For the area under the graph of a function f the test is y < f(x): the point is lower than the graph.

From hits to area

def estimate_pi(repetitions):
    hits = 0
    for i in range(repetitions):
        x = random.uniform(0, 1)
        y = random.uniform(0, 1)
        if in_circle(x, y):
            hits = hits + 1
    return hits / repetitions * 4

print(estimate_pi(100000))

One possible output:

3.14124

The points fall equally densely everywhere in the square of 1 by 1. The share of points inside the quarter circle is therefore the part of the square that the quarter circle takes up. The square has area 1, so the share of hits is at once the area of the quarter circle. A whole circle with radius 1 has area pi; so times 4 gives an estimate of pi.

A different rectangle

def f(x):
    return x ** 2

def estimate_area(repetitions):
    hits = 0
    for i in range(repetitions):
        x = random.uniform(0, 2)
        y = random.uniform(0, 4)
        if y < f(x):
            hits = hits + 1
    return hits / repetitions * 8

print(estimate_area(100000))

One possible output:

2.6652

This estimates the area under the parabola between x = 0 and x = 2. The rectangle is 2 wide and 4 high, so it has area 8. The share of hits times 8 gives the area under the graph.

The share of hits is not yet an area: don't forget to multiply by the area of the rectangle. The rectangle also has to enclose the whole shape. Here it has to be 4 high, because f(2) = 4; with a lower rectangle you miss a part.

That is how you measure an area for which you know no formula, with only randomness and a counter.
