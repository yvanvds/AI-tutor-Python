Guess a number between 1 and 100 when all you hear is "higher" or "lower": you pick the middle each time and keep the half where the number is. Bisection searches for a zero in exactly that way.

Trapping the zero

def f(x):
    return x ** 3 - x - 1

a = 1
b = 2
print(f(a) * f(b) < 0)

Output:

True

f(1) is negative and f(2) is positive. So somewhere between 1 and 2 the graph has to cross the axis: the zero is trapped. Two numbers with a different sign have a negative product, hence the test f(a) * f(b) < 0.

One step

m = (a + b) / 2
if f(a) * f(m) < 0:
    b = m
else:
    a = m
print(a, b)

Output:

1 1.5

The midpoint m is 1.5, and f(1.5) is positive. So the sign change is between a and m: the right half drops out and m becomes the new b. Had f(m) been negative, the sign change would be between m and b and m would become the new a. After one step the interval is half as wide and the zero is still inside it.

Repeating until the interval is narrow enough

a = 1
b = 2
while b - a > 0.001:
    m = (a + b) / 2
    if f(a) * f(m) < 0:
        b = m
    else:
        a = m
print(round((a + b) / 2, 4))

Output:

1.3247

The loop goes on as long as the interval is wider than the tolerance. The midpoint is calculated again in every iteration, and per step only one of the two limits moves. The midpoint of the last interval is the answer: it is less than half a tolerance away from the real zero.

How many steps?

Every step halves the width: 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, and so on. After 3 steps the interval is 0.125 wide, after 10 steps about 0.001. How many steps you need depends only on the width you start with and the tolerance, not on the function.

Bisection only works with a sign change between a and b. The function x² - 1 has two zeros between -2 and 2, but is positive at both limits: without a sign change the method doesn't know which half to keep.

Making the steps visible

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def bisection_midpoints(a, b, tolerance):
    midpoints = []
    while b - a > tolerance:
        m = (a + b) / 2
        midpoints.append(m)
        if f(a) * f(m) < 0:
            b = m
        else:
            a = m
    return midpoints

midpoints = bisection_midpoints(1, 2, 0.01)
print(len(midpoints))

heights = []
for m in midpoints:
    heights.append(0)
xs = []
ys = []
for i in range(51):
    x = 1 + i * 0.02
    xs.append(x)
    ys.append(f(x))
plt.plot(xs, ys)
plt.scatter(midpoints, heights, color="red")
plt.grid()
plt.show()

Output:

7

The function stores every midpoint in a list and gives it back; the main script draws. The red points are on the horizontal axis, which is why heights is a list of zeros of the same length. The first point is at 1.5, the next at 1.25, and after that they creep closer and closer to the place where the graph crosses the axis.

With a sign change and a loop that halves, you find every zero as accurately as you want.
