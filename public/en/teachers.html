For teachers

What the app does for you as a teacher

The tutor practises Python with every student individually. You decide what is learned, follow the class during the lesson and get a well-founded grade proposal for the report card.

On this page you read what the app can do, how the tutor decides what to ask, what the app keeps and whether you can use it in your own school today.

Today only for SMA Arcadia The app currently only works with the school accounts of SMA Arcadia. What you need to use it in your own school, you read under In your own school.

The course

The course in your own hands

What the tutor asks and explains comes from what you write in the app. You don't need code for that. You'll find it in the sidebar, under Teacher.

Goals

Main goals and subgoals in a tree, in the order in which students work through them. Every subgoal has learning objectives: small things a student needs to be able to do, like predicting what an if/else prints.

For a subgoal you write teaching tips for the tutor and link the lesson. You write the title and description in Dutch; an English translation can be added. The learning objectives are in the goals file you export and import.

Lesson content

For every subgoal, a lesson the student reads, with examples they can run right away. You upload the lesson as HTML and see beside it how the student sees it. A student asks the tutor questions about the lesson in the chat beside it.

Every lesson has a Dutch source and can get an English translation. If you change the Dutch lesson, the app tells you the translation is out of date.

Instructions

Here you write what the tutor does and how it writes: how it asks a question, assesses an answer, gives a hint or writes a status report. They are plain texts, with placeholders like {subgoal} and {teachingTips} that the app fills in.

A few rules are fixed in the app: which language the tutor answers in, and that it doesn't give away the solution when a student asks something during an exercise.

Questions

A new question from the tutor goes into the question bank as soon as the first student answers it correctly. After that, the tutor can ask it again, without any wait. Open questions are not kept.

If a question has ten answers or more and fewer than half of them are right, the app hides it by itself. What is wrong or unclear, you hide or delete yourself. A student can show you the ID at the top of every exercise, like #3fa91c.

In class

Following the class

You see every student's progress, and during the lesson the app tells you who is getting stuck.

Students

All your students in one list, with their class, their current goal and their progress. Click a student for the details: progress per goal and subgoal, a chart of progress over time, and the status reports the tutor writes after every finished subgoal.

Classes

Every class with its students and its lessons per week: day, start and end. You choose a student's class under Students. From the lesson times, the app knows which exercises fall within the lesson.

Alerts

Has a student worked on a subgoal for at least 25 minutes, and are half or more of their latest answers not right? Then the Students page shows an alert for them: Stuck without progress. That way you see it while the lesson is still going.

If a student answers a learning objective mostly right at home and mostly wrong in the lesson after that, the app reports: Class work contradicts home work. Alerts don't change the grade, and the student doesn't see them.

Usage

At the top of Students you see the token usage per class: what the tutor used at the AI model in the last 30 days for that class's exercises. Which model the tutor uses for the whole school, you choose in Options.

Badges

Students earn badges in their Trophy case: for effort, for right answers, and a class podium for the first three in the class to finish a topic. Badges don't count towards the grade.

Three badges you award yourself, for things the app can't see, with Award badge in a student's details: Fault finder, Helping hand and Good question!

Behind the scenes

How the tutor decides

The app decides what gets asked. The language model writes the question and assesses the answer. This is how that works, in plain language.

  1. An estimate for every learning objective

    For every learning objective, the app keeps track of how sure it is that the student can do it, and how many answers that rests on. After every exercise, that estimate is adjusted.

    A right answer to a hard question weighs more than one to an easy question. A mistake on an easy question weighs more than a mistake on a hard one. Picking the right option in a multiple-choice question counts only half.

    Recent answers count most: someone who first answers wrong five times and then right four times can do it now. What hasn't come up for months slowly sinks back.

  2. The next question

    Of the subgoal's learning objectives that haven't been demonstrated yet, the tutor picks the one with the lowest estimate, but never the same one twice in a row. The kind of question fits the learning objective: someone who needs to learn to write code gets code to write or complete, not a multiple-choice question. When the student is struggling, the tutor picks the gentlest kind of question. Sometimes the question comes from the question bank.

    Every student has one level: easy, medium or hard. Everyone starts at medium. After a series of good answers it goes up a step, after a series of weaker ones down a step. Going down is quicker than going up. The level carries over to the next subgoal.

  3. When a learning objective is demonstrated

    The estimate has to be high enough and rest on enough answers. On top of that, it takes: at least two fully right answers to questions about that learning objective itself, at least one of which is not multiple choice, and at least one good answer at the student's level or higher. So clicking the right option twice is not enough.

  4. When a subgoal is done

    A subgoal is done when all of its learning objectives have been demonstrated. Optional learning objectives don't count for that.

    If a learning objective still doesn't work out after six questions, the tutor moves on anyway. The student doesn't notice, but you get an alert. That learning objective comes back later as a check question, one step easier at first.

  5. Old material comes back

    A demonstrated learning objective from an earlier subgoal that hasn't come up for a long time gets a warm-up question at the start of a session. A learning objective that wasn't demonstrated yet comes back as a check question.

  6. In class counts more

    An answer during the lesson weighs a quarter more than one at home. During the lesson means: within the class's lesson times under Classes, from 10 minutes before until 10 minutes after the lesson.

    There is no button: the time decides, not the place. For a class without lesson times, everything counts as home.

Want to know more? The rules are written out in full, with every number, in three documents on GitHub: what the app tracks for every student, how the tutor decides and the grade formula (in Dutch).

Grading

From exercises to a report card grade

From what the students demonstrated, the app calculates a grade proposal out of 100. You decide in advance what counts, and you sign off. The AI never chooses the grade.

Milestones

A milestone for every report period: the subgoals that should be mastered by then, the period and the expected level. For every learning objective you mark whether a student who only just passes masters it. That is the core; the rest is extension.

The whole core at the expected level gives 50 out of 100. Above that, the extension counts, and what was demonstrated at hard.

Grade formula

The formula is public: two students with the same data get the same proposal. A demonstrated learning objective keeps counting, even if the estimate drops later. So working on can't lower the grade.

Your students read the same explanation in the app, under Grade formula. Some of its numbers are still provisional: they will be adjusted after a comparison with grades the teacher gave the usual way.

Reports

Choose a milestone and a class, and click Generate reports. The app calculates the proposal for every student and has the AI write a justification for it, from the status reports and the progress.

You read it through, rewrite where needed, adjust the grade with a reason and sign off. With Release, all signed-off reports of the milestone go to the students at once. They read their grade and the explanation under My reports.

Data

What the app keeps, and what goes to OpenAI

What the app keeps about your students, where that is, how long it stays, and what the language model gets to see.

Signing in

Students and teachers sign in with their Microsoft school account (Microsoft Entra). From it, the app gets the name and the email address. Whoever has the teacher role in Entra also sees the Teacher part of the app.

When a student runs code, it runs on their own laptop: Python is in the app.

What the app keeps

Everything is in one database in Azure Cosmos DB, in Microsoft's cloud. For every student:

  • name, email address, class, level and badges;
  • for every learning objective the estimate, and for every subgoal the progress and how it changed;
  • for every exercise a short record: which learning objectives, right or wrong, the difficulty, the time, in class or at home;
  • separately, the question, the answer and the feedback of every exercise;
  • the status reports, the grade proposals and the released reports;
  • the code the student saves in the Playground.

Besides that, it holds the goals, the lessons, the instructions and the question bank.

How long

The question, the answer and the feedback of an exercise stay until the end of the school year (1 July by default). Then the database deletes them by itself. They are there to see where a student got stuck; you can't open them in the app itself yet. The student reads in Options that they are kept.

The rest stays: there is no automatic clean-up. A student can reset their own progress in Options, completely or for one goal. Then their estimates, the record of every exercise and their questions and answers disappear too. The question bank stays from one school year to the next.

What goes to OpenAI

A language model from OpenAI writes the questions, assesses the answers and gives feedback. For that, it receives:

  • your instructions, with what the placeholders put into them: the title of the goal and the subgoal, the learning objectives and your teaching tips;
  • the conversation of the exercise: the question, the student's answer or code, and what the tutor has already said;
  • the questions the student just got, so the tutor doesn't repeat them;
  • for a question about the lesson: the text of that lesson;
  • for a status report: the student's conversation with the tutor.

The student's name, email address and class are not sent. What a student types does get sent, exactly as written.

One exception: for the justification of a grade proposal, the model gets the student's first name (or the email address when there is no first name), together with the numbers of the proposal and the status reports of the period.

In your own school

Can you use the app today?

Not just like that. The app you download on this site only works with the school accounts of SMA Arcadia. With an account from another school, you won't get past signing in.

Build it yourself

Want the app in your school now? Then you build your own version, with your school's Microsoft accounts, your own database and your own OpenAI account. You don't have to write code, but you do work in the Azure portal and in PowerShell.

The README on GitHub describes it step by step.

Without a school account

A version you can use without a school account is in preparation. You can follow it in #261 on GitHub.

A question?

Have a question or an idea? Open an issue on GitHub. There you can also see what is being worked on now.
